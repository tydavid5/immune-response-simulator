Neutrophil — the first responder

Arrives within minutes to hours. It recognises general danger patterns, swallows bacteria, then dies doing it. Pus is mostly dead neutrophils.

Deeper detail · The lymph node journey

Where the adaptive decision is actually made — watch the highlight move as you play

1. CaptureDendritic cell grabs antigen at the infection site.
2. TravelIt rides lymph vessels to the nearest lymph node — hours to a day.
3. PresentationAntigen shown on MHC to thousands of passing T cells.
4. Clonal expansionThe one matching T/B cell copies itself thousands of times.
5. Affinity & memoryB cells improve antibody fit; some cells retire as memory.
Complement

30+ blood proteins that tag microbes (opsonisation), recruit cells, and punch holes in bacterial membranes. Fast, no learning needed.

Why TB and malaria are hard

Both hide inside your own cells, where antibodies cannot reach. Only T cells — and time — can contain them. That is why immunity there is partial and slow.

IgM → IgG → IgA

IgM arrives first (big, quick). IgG follows (precise, long-lived, crosses placenta). IgA guards wet surfaces — gut and airways — so cholera and flu meet it first.

One infected person meets a town, a country, a continent. Change vaccination coverage and watch the epidemic curve bend — or explode. The herd-immunity threshold is not opinion, it is arithmetic: threshold = 1 − 1/R₀. The more contagious the disease, the higher the line everyone must cross together.

The two systems

Innate vs Adaptive — in one minute

Innate is born ready. Fast, blunt, same every time. Skin, mucus, neutrophils, macrophages, complement, fever.

Adaptive learns. Slow first time (5–10 days), precise, remembers. Every vaccine is a lesson for this system.

At population scale the same logic repeats: a naive population is a primary response (slow, severe); a vaccinated population is a secondary response (fast, mild). Herd immunity is memory, shared.

Why this matters in Nigeria — and everywhere

From one body to a programme

  • Malaria, Lassa, TB exploit the lag before adaptive immunity kicks in.
  • Measles at 9 months is timed for when maternal antibodies fade.
  • Coverage gaps are local: a country average can hide an unprotected state, school or city — which is why the table above grades place by place.
  • Discussion prompt: Is it fair that your protection depends on your neighbours vaccinating? Where does herd immunity help people who cannot be vaccinated?

Simplified educational models — body curves are illustrative, based on typical primary/secondary kinetics (innate peak 1–3 days, primary IgG peak 10–14 days, secondary response 1–3 days); population curves use a basic SIR model with textbook R₀ ranges (measles 12–18, pertussis ~12–17, polio 5–7, ancestral COVID-19 ~2.5–3, seasonal flu ~1.3–1.5).
